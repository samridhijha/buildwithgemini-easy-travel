WORLD TRAVEL HANDBOOK & GLOBAL CONCIERGE GUIDE
===================================================

CHAPTER 1: GLOBAL EMERGENCY PROTOCOLS & PASSORPORT LOSS
-------------------------------------------------------
Emergency Phone Numbers by Region:
- European Union (EU): 112 (Universal Emergency Number - works from any mobile phone even without SIM card).
- United States & Canada: 911 (Police, Fire, Ambulance).
- United Kingdom: 999 or 112.
- Japan: 110 (Police), 119 (Fire & Ambulance). English-speaking helpline available via Japan Helpline: 0570-000-911.
- Thailand: 1155 (Tourist Police - English speaking), 191 (General Emergency).
- Australia: 000.

Lost or Stolen Passport Procedure:
1. File a police report immediately at the nearest local police station and obtain an official written report copy.
2. Contact your home country's nearest Embassy or Consulate to apply for an Emergency Travel Certificate (ETC) or replacement passport.
3. Keep physical photocopies and encrypted digital scans of your passport bio page, driver's license, and travel insurance policy stored separately from your physical documents.

Travel Insurance Claims:
- Always keep itemized receipts for all emergency medical care, delayed baggage purchases, and flight cancellation notes.
- Notify your insurance provider within 24 hours of any incident to ensure claim eligibility.


CHAPTER 2: GLOBAL POWER PLUG, VOLTAGE & TECH ADVISORY
------------------------------------------------------
Voltage Compatibility:
- North America & Japan: 100V - 120V (60Hz / 50Hz).
- Europe, UK, Asia, Africa, Australia: 220V - 240V (50Hz).
- Dual-voltage electronics (laptops, smartphones, camera chargers marked '100-240V, 50/60Hz') only require a physical plug adapter, not a voltage transformer. Hairdryers and curling irons usually require a step-down converter unless dual-voltage.

Plug Type Guide:
- Type A & B: USA, Canada, Mexico, Japan (2-prong ungrounded or 3-prong).
- Type C, E, & F: Continental Europe, South America, Asia (2 round pins).
- Type G: United Kingdom, Ireland, Singapore, Hong Kong, UAE (3 rectangular prongs with internal fuse).
- Type I: Australia, New Zealand, China, Argentina (2 or 3 slanted flat blades).

Connectivity Tips:
- Purchase an eSIM (Airalo, Nomad, Holafly) before departure for instant data activation upon landing.
- In Japan, Pocket Wi-Fi rentals provide unlimited high-speed data for multiple devices (battery lasts 8-12 hours).


CHAPTER 3: AIRLINE BAGGAGE & AIRPORT SECURITY LAWS
--------------------------------------------------
TSA & International Security Liquids Rule (3-1-1):
- All liquids, gels, aerosol cans, and pastes in carry-on bags must be in containers of 3.4 ounces (100ml) or smaller.
- All containers must fit into a single 1-quart transparent zip-top plastic bag.

Lithium-Ion Battery Regulations:
- Spare lithium-ion batteries, power banks, and portable phone chargers MUST be packed in carry-on luggage. They are strictly prohibited in checked luggage due to fire safety hazards.
- Power banks are generally limited to 100 watt-hours (Wh) or approximately 27,000 mAh.

Luggage Forwarding (Takkyubin in Japan):
- Japan offers nationwide door-to-door luggage transport (Yamato Transport / Hands-Free Travel). Send heavy suitcases directly from Narita/Haneda airport to your hotel or between cities (e.g. Tokyo to Kyoto) for approx $15-$25 per bag, allowing hassle-free travel on bullet trains.


CHAPTER 4: CULTURAL ETIQUETTE & TIPPING STANDARDS
-------------------------------------------------
Tipping Guidelines by Destination:
- United States & Canada: Tipping is expected and customary. Restaurants: 18% - 22% of total pre-tax bill. Taxis: 15% - 20%. Hotel Porters: $2 - $5 per bag.
- Continental Europe (France, Italy, Germany, Spain): Service charge (Service Compris / Coperto) is often included. Leaving small change or 5% - 10% cash for exceptional service is appreciated but not mandatory.
- United Kingdom: Restaurant bills often add a 12.5% discretionary service charge. Pubs do not expect tips at the bar.
- Japan & South Korea: Tipping is strictly NOT part of the culture and can be considered impolite or confusing. Excellent service is standard and included.
- Southeast Asia (Thailand, Vietnam, Bali): Tipping is not traditional, but rounding up taxi fares or leaving 10% at Western-style tourist restaurants is welcomed.

Local Cultural Norms:
- Japan: Bow slightly when greeting. Never walk while eating street food; eat near the stall. Remove shoes when entering homes, ryokans, or temples with tatami mats. On escalators, stand on the left in Tokyo (right in Osaka). Keep quiet on public transportation and mute cell phone ringers (Manner Mode).
- Thailand: Never touch someone's head (sacred body part) or point feet at people or Buddha statues (unclean). Cover shoulders and knees when visiting temples (Grand Palace, Wat Pho). Show utmost respect for the Royal Family.
- Italy & Europe: Respect church dress codes (cover shoulders and knees when entering St. Peter's Basilica or the Duomo). Order espresso or macchiato after 11 AM—cappuccinos are considered strictly breakfast drinks.


CHAPTER 5: DESTINATION INSIDER TIPS & TRANSPORTATION
---------------------------------------------------
TOKYO, JAPAN:
- Transportation: Buy a digital Suica or Pasmo card on Apple Wallet / Google Pay for instant tap-in transit on subways, buses, and payment at convenience stores (Konbini like 7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart).
- Bullet Train (Shinkansen): Book baggage reservation seats if traveling with oversized luggage (dimensions exceeding 160cm). Mount Fuji views: request seat E (ordinary car) or seat D (green car) when traveling from Tokyo towards Kyoto/Osaka.

PARIS, FRANCE:
- Public Transit: Use the Navigo Easy card or contactless phone tap instead of paper metro tickets.
- Sightseeing: Book Louvre Museum and Eiffel Tower tickets 2 to 3 months in advance online. Free entry to national museums occurs on the first Sunday of each month (off-peak season).
- Safety & Scams: Beware of common street scams around Sacré-Cœur and Eiffel Tower (the string/bracelet trick, petition signers, gold ring scam, and pickpockets on Metro Line 1 and 9).

ROME, ITALY:
- Free Drinking Water: Carry a reusable water bottle and fill up for free at any of Rome's 2,500 historic public water fountains known as "Nasoni" (big nose fountains).
- Transport & Driving: Avoid driving in central Rome due to ZTL (Zona a Traffico Limitato) camera-enforced fine zones.

BANGKOK, THAILAND:
- Transport: Use ride-hailing apps like Grab or Bolt for upfront fare quotes, avoiding unmetered taxi driver haggling. When taking a traditional yellow-green taxi, insist on "Meter please".
- Food & Hydration: Drink bottled or filtered water only (avoid tap water). Street food stalls with long local queues are generally very fresh and safe.

NEW YORK CITY, USA:
- Subway System: Tap to pay with any contactless credit card, debit card, or phone (OMNY system) directly at subway turnstiles. Weekly fare capping automatically applies after 12 paid rides per week.
- Broadway Shows: Get discounted same-day theatre tickets at TKTS booths (Times Square, Lincoln Center) or enter digital lotteries (TodayTix app).
- Airport Transfers: JFK Airport to Manhattan: Take the JFK AirTrain to Jamaica Station ($8.50) then LIRR train ($5-$10) or E Subway line ($2.90) for a fast 45-minute trip, bypassing highway traffic jams.
